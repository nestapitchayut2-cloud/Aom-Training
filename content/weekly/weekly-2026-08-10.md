## 🗣 Role-play cards

### Card 1 — Perioral rash (declining a sale, with a reason)

**Ness plays:** Hannah, 26.

> "Hi, can I get some hydrocortisone cream? I've got a rash around my mouth."

- If asked how long: *"About six weeks now."*
- If asked what she has already used: *"My flatmate gave me her cream — betamethasone, I think. It cleared up fast but comes straight back worse when I stop."*
- Anything else: uses sunscreen only, no other medicines, not pregnant.

**Marking criteria**

1. Elicits the history of potent steroid use obtained from someone else.
2. **Declines to supply a steroid and explains that the steroid itself is driving the rebound.**
3. Warns that stopping will look worse before it looks better — otherwise she will restart it.
4. Refers to the GP with a clear timeframe.

### Card 2 — SGLT2 inhibitor + NSAID + fasting

**Ness plays:** Peter, 58, collecting metformin and empagliflozin.

> "…and I'll grab some ibuprofen for my knee, thanks."

- If asked about diet: *"I started keto and 20-hour fasting two weeks ago. Down 4 kilos already!"*
- If asked about symptoms: *"A bit light-headed when I stand up. Thought that was just the diet."*
- If asked who advised it: read about it himself, has not told his doctor.

**Marking criteria**

1. Links empagliflozin + keto/fasting to **euglycaemic DKA** — ketoacidosis with normal blood glucose.
2. Recognises NSAID + SGLT2 inhibitor + dehydration as a risk of acute kidney injury.
3. Gives sick-day rules and red-flag symptoms that mean going straight to the emergency department.
4. **Offers an alternative to ibuprofen** rather than only refusing it.

## 🔢 Pharmaceutical calculations

1. Prepare 500 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride from a 23.4% stock. What volume of stock is required?
2. Amoxicillin 40 mg/kg/day in three divided doses for a child weighing 14 kg. The suspension is 250 mg/5 mL. What volume is given per dose?
3. One litre is to be infused over 8 hours using a giving set delivering 20 drops/mL. Calculate the rate in drops per minute.
4. Adrenaline is labelled 1:1000. Express this as mg/mL, then state the volume equivalent to a 0.5 mg dose.
5. Prepare 100 g of a 2% w/w cream from a 10% w/w stock and base. How much of each is needed?

**Worked answers** *(try them first)*

| # | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 500 × 0.9 ÷ 23.4 | **19.2 mL** of stock, made up to 500 mL with diluent |
| 2 | 14 × 40 = 560 mg/day ÷ 3 = 186.7 mg → ÷ 250 × 5 | **3.7 mL** (≈3.75 mL in practice) |
| 3 | 1000 mL × 20 = 20 000 drops ÷ 480 min | **42 drops/min** |
| 4 | 1:1000 means 1 g in 1000 mL | **1 mg/mL**, so 0.5 mg = **0.5 mL** |
| 5 | Alligation: 100 × 2 ÷ 10 | **20 g stock + 80 g base** |

## 💊 Clinical summary — HFrEF: the four pillars

Guideline-directed therapy for heart failure with reduced ejection fraction rests on four drug classes started early and titrated together rather than sequentially: an **ARNI or ACE inhibitor**, a **beta-blocker**, an **MRA**, and an **SGLT2 inhibitor**.

Counselling points worth rehearsing aloud:

- A modest early rise in creatinine after starting an ARNI or an MRA is expected and is **not** a reason to stop treatment.
- Beta-blockers may worsen fatigue for two to three weeks before any benefit is felt.
- Sacubitril/valsartan requires a **36-hour washout** from any ACE inhibitor because of the risk of angioedema.
- MRAs require potassium monitoring, and patients should avoid potassium-based salt substitutes.

## ⚖️ 🇦🇺 Australian law — the Poisons Standard (SUSMP)

Medicines in Australia are classified by the **Poisons Standard (SUSMP)**, which is then given legal force by each state and territory. That split is the trap: the schedules are national, but the day-to-day requirements differ by jurisdiction.

| Schedule | Name | What gets examined |
|---|---|---|
| **S2** | Pharmacy Medicine | May be sold in a pharmacy; a pharmacist does not have to hand it over personally |
| **S3** | Pharmacist Only | **The pharmacist must be personally involved in the sale** and must counsel the patient |
| **S4** | Prescription Only | Requires a prescription |
| **S8** | Controlled Drug | Stored in a safe and entered in the drugs register, which must reconcile |

**Compared with Ireland:** the underlying logic is similar (general sale / pharmacist-supervised / prescription only / controlled), but Ireland uses the Misuse of Drugs Regulations with Schedules 1–5 while Australia uses S1–S10, and **the numbers do not map onto each other**.

> Learn them as a pair: Ireland **Sch 2** = the tightly controlled drugs · Australia **S8** = the tightly controlled drugs

## 📖 Glossary

- **rebound** — อาการเด้งกลับแรงกว่าเดิมหลังหยุดยา
- **elicit** — ซักจนได้ข้อมูลออกมา
- **potent** — (ยา) ความแรงสูง
- **euglycaemic** — ระดับน้ำตาลในเลือดปกติ
- **sick-day rules** — คำแนะนำการปรับยาช่วงเจ็บป่วยเฉียบพลัน
- **red flag** — สัญญาณอันตรายที่ต้องส่งต่อทันที
- **giving set** — ชุดให้สารน้ำทางหลอดเลือด
- **alligation** — วิธีคำนวณผสมความเข้มข้นสองระดับ
- **titrate** — ค่อยๆ ปรับขนาดยาขึ้นทีละขั้น
- **washout period** — ช่วงเว้นระยะก่อนเปลี่ยนยา
- **angioedema** — อาการบวมใต้ผิวหนัง/เยื่อบุ
- **reconcile** — (บัญชียา) ตรวจนับให้ตรงกับที่บันทึกไว้
- **jurisdiction** — เขตอำนาจตามกฎหมาย (รัฐ/ประเทศ)
